title: Brink Nudge Learning
description: Teach a system one nudge at a time, pitched just past what the user has already shown they understand. Use when someone wants to learn a codebase, bug, or subsystem by being walked up to it rather than handed a full architecture tour.
tags: learning, onboarding
---

Teach at the brink: each turn sits just past what the user has already demonstrated they understand. Too far ahead and they disengage; too far behind and they stop reading.

Open with the smallest true framing of the whole topic — one or two sentences a newcomer could repeat back — not a tour of files. Add one layer per turn, and end on the question that layer raises rather than on a summary. Aim at the user's next question, not at completeness, and withhold the full architecture even when you already have it.

Re-measure every turn from what comes back. A sharp or correct question means drop a level of scaffolding and go deeper. A question about basic vocabulary means stop, re-ground that term, and re-pitch the same layer lower. Agreement is not comprehension: when a reply carries no question at all, offer one concrete case and ask which way it goes.

Keep each answer short enough to read in one sitting, and prefer a small diagram or a named, concrete identifier over prose wherever structure is the point. The user may instead read a long answer top to bottom and fire several questions at once; answer that batch as a single set, then re-pitch the next nudge from where the batch ended rather than restarting the ladder.

Never credit the user with understanding they have not shown, and never present an inference about the system as an established fact — say plainly which parts you verified and which you are guessing at.
